### Long-Term Research Vision

#### Safe Whole-Body Autonomy Around People

Over the longer term, I want to extend safety-aware locomotion toward coordinated whole-body control, involving manipulation, and contact-aware task execution in dynamic human environments. The challenge is to maintain balance while deciding when to avoid contact, when to allow controlled and compliant contact, and when to recover or stop, without violating physical constraints or abandoning on-going task.


### Problem Statement: Safe Legged Locomotion in Human Environments

Legged locomotion, safety, and interaction between humans and robots are the intersections of my research interests. While modern robots can increasingly track trajectories and recover from disturbance in controlled settings, the central challenge is not simply getting a robot to walk. When operating in uncertain, human-populated environments, it is creating a safe, resilient, and socially aware system. According to [Kai Williams](https://www.understandingai.org/p/why-humanoid-robots-wont-catch-up#footnote-anchor-3) at [Understanding AI](https://www.understandingai.org/p/), the biggest engineering challenge for humanoid robots is safety. Most current legged humanoids have to actively maintain balance. If, for whatever reason, the motors in the legs stop running, the robot will fall down, potentially injuring people in the process. This shows that real-world failure cases of humanoid robots reveal a crucial gap confirming that locomotion is not only a control problem, but also a safety and interaction problem in human settings.
 My initial focus is to study how legged humanoids can navigate clutter, recover from unanticipated disturbances, and remain stable and predictable around people. I want to combine physically grounded dynamics and control with safety constraints and interaction-aware design, moving beyond walking in idealized conditions toward reliable locomotion in shared spaces.
